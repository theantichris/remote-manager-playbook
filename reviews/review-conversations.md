# Review Conversations

Performance reviews are an opportunity to reflect on accomplishments, recognize growth, discuss challenges, and align on what comes next.

I don't believe a performance review should be a surprise. We should already be having regular conversations about performance, expectations, feedback, and career development throughout the year.

The formal review gives us an opportunity to step back, look at the bigger picture, and make sure we understand where things stand and where we want to go.

**The goal isn't simply to evaluate performance. It's to recognize contributions, support growth, and help people succeed.**

## Continuous Feedback

Performance reviews shouldn't be the first time someone hears about something they're doing well or something they need to improve.

I believe feedback is most useful when it's timely, specific, and actionable.

We should use our regular [1:1s](../meetings/one-on-ones.md) to discuss performance, celebrate accomplishments, address challenges, and identify opportunities for growth.

By the time we have a formal review conversation, we should already have a shared understanding of what's going well and what needs attention.

That doesn't mean there won't be differences in perspective or new insights. It means we shouldn't save important feedback for review season.

## Preparing for the Conversation

A meaningful review requires thoughtful preparation from both of us.

### My Preparation

Before the conversation, I'll:

1. Review your goals, responsibilities, and expectations for the review period.
2. Reflect on your accomplishments, contributions, and impact.
3. Gather specific examples that support my feedback.
4. Consider the challenges you faced and the context in which you were working.
5. Identify strengths, growth opportunities, and areas where I can provide better support.
6. Review feedback we've discussed throughout the period.
7. Prepare questions and topics I'd like us to explore together.

I want my feedback to be fair, specific, and based on observable behaviors and outcomes rather than assumptions or personal preferences.

It's also important to consider the opportunities and constraints someone has had. People shouldn't be evaluated solely on outcomes beyond their control.

### Your Preparation

I encourage you to reflect on:

- What accomplishments are you most proud of?
- What impact did your work have?
- What challenges did you face?
- What did you learn?
- Where have you grown?
- What would you do differently?
- What opportunities or responsibilities would you like to explore?
- What support do you need from me?

You don't need to prepare a presentation or defend every decision you've made. I want us to have a thoughtful conversation about your work and development.

## Scheduling and Sharing Reviews

I'll make sure we have enough dedicated time for the conversation without feeling rushed.

I typically prefer to schedule review conversations separately from our regular 1:1s or extend our existing meeting when that makes sense.

I'll communicate what to expect and, whenever the review process allows it, share the written review ahead of time so you have an opportunity to read and reflect before we meet.

We'll follow our organization's requirements for submitting, sharing, and documenting formal reviews.

## Having the Conversation

I want review conversations to feel collaborative, not like I'm delivering a verdict.

### 1. Recognize Accomplishments

Start with what went well.

- What did you accomplish?
- What impact did you have on the team, our customers, or the business?
- What strengths did you demonstrate?
- What contributions might not be immediately visible?

It's important to recognize not only what someone delivered but also how they helped others succeed.

Mentoring, improving processes, supporting teammates, and reducing friction can be just as valuable as delivering individual projects.

### 2. Discuss Challenges and Growth Opportunities

Talk openly about where things could have gone better.

Focus on specific examples, their impact, and what we can learn from them.

I want to understand your perspective, including any circumstances I may not have been aware of.

When performance needs to improve, we should be clear about the expectations and what meaningful improvement looks like.

The goal isn't to avoid difficult conversations. It's to have them respectfully, constructively, and with a shared understanding of what needs to change.

### 3. Invite Your Perspective

I don't expect my assessment to capture every detail of your experience.

Some questions I like to ask:

- Is there anything you'd like me to clarify?
- Is there anything I misunderstood?
- Is there something important I left out?
- Do you see anything differently?
- What feedback do you have for me?

I want you to feel comfortable challenging my perspective and sharing information I may have missed.

We don't have to agree on every assessment, but we should understand each other's perspectives and leave with clear expectations.

### 4. Discuss Career Development

A review should also help us look forward.

We'll revisit your [Career Development Plan](../templates/career-development-plan.md), reflect on your progress, and discuss what you'd like to work toward next.

Some questions worth exploring:

- What would you like to learn or improve?
- What kind of work would you like to do more of?
- What responsibilities or challenges interest you?
- What opportunities could help you grow?
- What support or feedback would be most valuable?

Career development doesn't always mean pursuing a promotion. I want to help you find work that's meaningful to you and opportunities that align with your interests and aspirations.

### 5. Agree on Next Steps

Before ending the conversation, we'll make sure we understand what comes next.

That might include:

- Continuing to build on existing strengths.
- Identifying one or two meaningful growth priorities.
- Clarifying performance expectations.
- Finding opportunities to practice new skills.
- Agreeing on actions or support I can provide.
- Scheduling additional conversations when needed.

We should leave with a clear understanding of our commitments to each other.

## Following Up

The review conversation shouldn't be the end of the process.

We'll follow up on commitments, revisit development goals, and discuss progress during our regular 1:1s.

If we identify something that needs attention, we shouldn't wait until the next review cycle to address it.

Likewise, we should continue recognizing accomplishments and providing feedback throughout the year.

The review should strengthen our ongoing conversations, not replace them.

## Documentation and Confidentiality

Formal performance reviews often contain sensitive information.

We'll follow our organization's policies for documenting, storing, and sharing reviews and related feedback.

Our shared 1:1 notes are useful for tracking development goals and agreed-upon actions, but confidential performance assessments should remain in the appropriate systems with the appropriate access controls.

## What Success Looks Like

A successful review conversation should leave us both with:

- A shared understanding of your contributions and impact.
- Clear expectations about performance.
- Recognition of your strengths and accomplishments.
- An understanding of opportunities for growth.
- Meaningful next steps and commitments.
- Confidence that we can continue having open, constructive conversations.

I want you to leave feeling heard, respected, and supported, even when we've discussed difficult feedback or areas that need improvement.

**A good performance review shouldn't tell you something you've never heard before. It should help us understand how far you've come and where we go next.**
