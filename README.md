# Remote Manager Playbook

## Hey, y'all

Working with a remote, distributed, and asynchronous team necessitates a handbook-first approach. Information should be easy to find, understand, and use without needing to schedule a meeting to get it.

Transparency is key. I want you to understand how I work, what I value, and what you can expect from me. I also want us to be clear about goals, decisions, and responsibilities so nobody has to guess.

This playbook is a collection of living documents. I'll update it as my approach evolves. If something is missing, unclear, or no longer reflects how we work, let me know or open an issue or PR.

## My Role

I am not here to make sure you do your job, I am here to make sure you can do your job.

My role is to guide people, teams, and organizations toward their goals while helping them grow. I want my team to flourish in their lives, not just at work. I aim for you to feel healthy, fulfilled, productive, and confident in your ability to do great work.

That means helping us understand the outcomes we're trying to achieve, making trade-offs and decision boundaries clear, removing obstacles, and giving people the support and autonomy to own their work. I should be making it easier for you to succeed, not becoming another step you have to get through.

## What I Value Most

- **Holistic development.** We are not just employees, we're people. Our work life influences our personal life and vice versa. Strive for a healthy work-life cycle. Let your successes in one fuel the other.
- **Growth mindset.** I want to see you grow in your role, your career, and as a person. Work with me to set goals and find opportunities to reach them.
- **Outcomes and results.** Start with the result we want and work backward. How we get there should be open for discussion, experimentation, and learning.
- **Ownership and autonomy.** People closest to the work should have room to make decisions. Ownership means being accountable for an outcome, not doing everything yourself.
- **Collaboration.** We succeed as a team and with our partners. Share context, involve the right people, and avoid working in silos.
- **Feedback.** I want feedback early and often, including feedback for me. I am comfortable receiving it publicly, but you can always bring it to me privately.

## How I Lead

### Clarity before activity

I want us to understand why we're doing something, what success looks like, and what we are choosing not to do. Being busy is not the goal. Delivering something valuable is.

### Ownership without unnecessary layers

A project should have a clear directly responsible individual (DRI) who can explain its progress, decisions, risks, and next steps. The DRI does not have to do all the work. My job is to help establish the boundaries, access, and support needed for that person to lead. See [Project Ownership and Weekly Updates](process/project-ownership.md).

### Written communication by default

Writing things down makes it easier for distributed teams to stay informed and make decisions without constantly interrupting each other. I value concise updates with enough context, a clear ask, an owner, and a deadline when one matters. Async is a default, not a rule against talking. Sensitive feedback, difficult discussions, and problems that need rapid collaboration deserve a conversation.

### Trust with accountability

I don't need to be part of every decision. Make the decisions you have the context and authority to make, keep the people affected informed, and raise risks before they become surprises. If something goes wrong, I want us to understand what happened and improve the system, not discourage people from taking ownership.

### Coaching and growth

Our 1:1s should be useful to you, not just a status report for me. I'll ask questions, provide feedback, help remove obstacles, and look for opportunities to grow your skills and influence. I expect feedback in return.

## How I Operate

- My typical work hours are Monday through Friday, 08:00–16:00 Eastern. I am usually unavailable outside these hours, with work notifications turned off. If you have my personal number and need me, don't hesitate to text.
- Organization is crucial for me. I rely heavily on my calendar, notebooks, and task lists.
- I prefer an asynchronous message that tells me the context, what you need, and when you need it. If a conversation would be faster or more thoughtful, let's have one.

## My Expectations

- **Keep your word.** If something comes up that makes this impossible, let me know. Shit happens, let's figure it out together.
- **Be honest and transparent.** Surface problems, uncertainty, and changing expectations early.
- **Own outcomes and communicate progress.** Keep the people who depend on your work informed. Ask for help when you need it.
- **Continuously learn and grow.** Try things, seek feedback, and share what you learn.
- **Focus.** Limit your work in progress (WIP) and prioritize what matters. Finishing important work is more valuable than starting more things.
- **Protect your life outside work.** Prioritize your mental health, the people you care about, and the things you value.

## Personality Quirks

- I am quite forgetful. If I do not write something down, there's a good chance I'll forget it. If you don't see me make a note of something important, please say something.
- I have narcolepsy. If you see me yawn, I'm not bored, just tired. If you see me get up and move around, it's to maintain my attention and wakefulness.
- I like to think and process information before acting. If we are having a conversation and I'm quiet for a bit I am doing just that. I'll usually say something like "processing...".

## Personal Life

I live in Knoxville, TN with my wife, our 3 kids, our dog, and our cat. I love to read, swim, be in nature, play video games, read tarot, play the banjo, and ride my motorcycle. I don't love leaving my house.

## Audience and Scope

This playbook is for people who work with me, including reports, peers, and partners. It covers ways of working, leadership practices, strategy, and team collaboration. Technical documentation belongs with the systems and code it describes.

## Contributing

Issues and PRs are welcome. Keep the tone friendly, direct, and practical. Favor examples over theory. This is a public playbook, so please don't include private employee or company information.
