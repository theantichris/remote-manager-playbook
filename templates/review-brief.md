# Review Brief: {{title}}

When asking someone to review work, provide enough context for them to understand what they're reviewing, why it matters, and how they can help.

The goal is to make reviews productive without requiring unnecessary meetings or back-and-forth communication.

Keep it concise. Not every review needs an extensive document.

## What Needs Review

**Links:** [Documents, designs, PRs, or other materials]

**Context:** [What are we working on, and why does it matter?]

**Focus:** [Which parts specifically need attention?]

Make sure reviewers have access to the materials before requesting feedback.

## Feedback or Decision Needed

Be specific about what you need from the reviewer.

- What questions do you need answered?
- What risks, assumptions, or trade-offs need evaluation?
- Are you requesting feedback, approval, or a decision?
- Is there anything explicitly out of scope for this review?

**Decision Maker:** [If a decision is required, who has authority to make it?]

I want us to distinguish between the feedback being requested and all the feedback someone could potentially provide.

The goal isn't to collect every possible opinion. It's to get the information needed to make progress.

## Timing and Response

**Reviewers:** [Who needs to provide input?]

**Deadline:** [When is feedback needed, and why? Include the time zone when appropriate.]

**Response Format:** [Where and how should feedback be provided? For example, document comments, GitHub, Slack, or a conversation.]

**Next Step:** [Who will incorporate the feedback, communicate the decision, or move the work forward?]

Not every review needs an urgent deadline. Give people a reasonable amount of time based on the complexity and importance of the request.

---

A good review request should make it easy for people to provide useful feedback and understand what happens next.

**Ask for the feedback you need, not simply whatever feedback someone feels like giving.**
