# Team Charter: {{title}}

A team charter helps us establish a shared understanding of why our team exists, what we're responsible for, and how we want to work together.

I believe effective teams need clear goals, meaningful ownership, strong relationships, and the autonomy to make decisions.

This document should be created collaboratively by the team. It represents the commitments we're making to each other, not simply expectations imposed by leadership.

Keep it practical. The goal isn't to document every possible situation. It's to provide enough clarity that we can work together effectively and make good decisions independently.

### Directly Responsible Individuals

For significant projects or initiatives, identify a directly responsible individual (DRI).

The DRI owns the outcome and coordinates the work, but doesn't have to do everything themselves.

The purpose of clear ownership is to enable autonomy and collaboration, not create another layer of approval.

I believe sustainable teams accomplish more over time than teams that rely on continuous urgency or excessive workloads.

## Team Health

How will we know whether the team is operating effectively?

Consider a combination of outcomes and signals:

- Are we delivering meaningful value?
- Are our systems reliable and maintainable?
- Can we make progress without excessive coordination?
- Are priorities and ownership clear?
- Is our workload sustainable?
- Do people feel supported and able to contribute?
- Are we learning and improving?

No single metric tells us everything about team health.

See [Team States](../team/team-states.md) for additional guidance.

## Reviewing the Charter

A team charter should evolve as our responsibilities, membership, and ways of working change.

**Review cadence:** [When we'll revisit the charter]

Revisit it when:

- The team gains or loses significant responsibilities.
- Membership or leadership changes.
- Our goals or mission change.
- Working agreements are no longer effective.
- We identify recurring problems that require new commitments.

Changes should be discussed and agreed upon by the people affected.

The charter should reflect how we've agreed to work, not simply document how things happened in the past.
