# Decision Brief: {{title}}

A decision brief should make it easy for someone to understand what needs to be decided, why it matters, and what you recommend.

Keep it concise. Someone should be able to understand the decision in a few minutes without needing a meeting. Link to supporting documents when more detail is necessary.

## Context

What problem or opportunity are we addressing?

Why does this decision matter now? What happens if we don't make it?

Provide enough background for someone unfamiliar with the work to understand the situation.

## Facts and Unknowns

List the information relevant to making the decision.

Facts should be objective, verifiable, and supported by evidence. Use a numbered list so people can easily reference individual points during discussion.

1. [Relevant fact and supporting evidence.]
2. [Relevant fact and supporting evidence.]

**Unknowns and assumptions:**

- [What don't we know?]
- [What are we assuming to be true?]

Be clear about the difference between what we know and what we believe.

## Recommendation

What do you recommend, and why?

Explain how your proposal addresses the problem, what outcome you expect, and why you believe it's the best available approach.

Try to keep this to one or two paragraphs. Link to a larger document if additional detail is necessary.

**Make a recommendation rather than simply presenting options and asking someone else to figure everything out.**

## Risks and Reversibility

- What could go wrong?
- How would we recognize a problem?
- What could we do to reduce the risk?
- How easily could we reverse the decision?
- What would cause us to reconsider?

Not every decision requires extensive analysis. Reversible, low-risk decisions should generally be made quickly. Decisions with significant or difficult-to-reverse consequences deserve more consideration.

## Expected Outcome

What does success look like?

How will we know whether the decision achieved its intended outcome?

Identify measurable results or observable changes when appropriate.
