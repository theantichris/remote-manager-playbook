# 1:1s

Our 1:1s are dedicated time for you. They're an opportunity to talk about what's going well, what's challenging, where you want to grow, and how I can better support you.

These meetings aren't status updates. We have other ways to communicate project progress. I want our time together to focus on you as a person, your career, and anything that deserves a conversation.

## Cadence and Scheduling

You get to choose how frequently we meet. Some people prefer weekly 1:1s, while others find every other week works better. I'm comfortable with either, and we can adjust the cadence whenever your needs change.

I typically schedule 30 minutes, but we can extend that when necessary. We also don't need to fill the entire time if we've covered everything.

I consider these meetings important and try to avoid canceling them. If something comes up, let's find another time rather than skipping the conversation altogether.

If you need something between meetings, don't wait for our next 1:1. Reach out whenever you need me.

## Shared Agenda

We'll maintain a shared agenda where we can both add topics, questions, and notes.

I encourage you to add anything you'd like to discuss before we meet, but you don't need to prepare a formal agenda every time.

**You drive the conversation.** I may have things I'd like to discuss, but your priorities should come first.

This is your opportunity to raise concerns, share ideas, ask questions, discuss challenges, or simply talk through something that's on your mind.

## What We Talk About

I don't believe every 1:1 needs to follow a rigid structure. Different people have different needs, and those needs can change from week to week.

Here are some areas we'll typically cover.

### Personal Connection and Check-in

I want to know how you're doing, not just how your projects are going.

- How are you feeling about your workload and energy?
- What's been taking up most of your mental bandwidth?
- How are things going for you generally?

You don't have to share anything about your personal life that you're not comfortable sharing. My goal is to understand how you're doing and whether there's anything I can do to support you.

### Your Agenda

This is the most important part of our conversation.

- What's the most important thing you'd like to talk about?
- What's going well?
- Where are you experiencing friction or uncertainty?
- Are there decisions, challenges, or relationships you'd like help navigating?
- Is there anything you'd like to change about how we work together?

You don't need to have solutions or even fully formed questions. Sometimes talking through a problem is the best way to understand it.

### My Agenda

I'll bring topics when there's something I think we should discuss.

These may include:

- Feedback, both positive and constructive.
- Cross-functional collaboration and relationships.
- Opportunities to expand your responsibilities or impact.
- Career development and growth.
- Challenges or opportunities I've noticed.
- Commitments or conversations we need to follow up on.

I'll do my part to come prepared. I don't expect you to carry the entire responsibility for making our 1:1s valuable.

### Career Development

Your growth is important to me, and I want us to make time for it regularly.

We'll use your [Career Development Plan](../templates/career-development-plan.md) to explore where you want to go, identify opportunities, and make progress toward your goals.

Not every 1:1 needs to include a formal career development discussion, but I don't want us to save those conversations for performance reviews either.

Growth isn't always about promotion. It's about helping you develop the skills, confidence, and experience to do the work you find meaningful.

### Feedback

Feedback should go both ways.

I'll share feedback to help you recognize your strengths and identify opportunities to improve. I also want you to feel comfortable telling me what I'm doing well and where I could be a better manager.

You don't need to wait for a 1:1 to give or receive feedback, particularly when something needs attention.

## Wrapping Up

Before we finish, we'll make sure we're clear about any commitments or next steps.

A few questions I like to ask:

- Is there anything we didn't get to that needs more attention?
- What can I do differently to better support you?
- Are there any actions we need to take before our next meeting?

We'll capture important action items in our shared notes so neither of us has to rely on memory.

## When We Can't Meet

If we can't meet, we can use our shared agenda to exchange updates, ask questions, or follow up on outstanding items.

I'll aim to respond to asynchronous questions within one business day, and we'll address anything time-sensitive sooner.

However, asynchronous communication isn't always a replacement for a conversation. Some discussions deserve our full attention, particularly when they involve difficult feedback, personal challenges, or complex situations.

If we need to talk, we'll find the time.

## What I Want You to Get From Our 1:1s

I want you to leave our conversations feeling heard, supported, and more confident about what comes next.

Sometimes that means solving a problem. Sometimes it's identifying an opportunity, getting feedback, or simply having someone listen.

**Our 1:1s should work for you. If they aren't providing value, let's talk about what we can change.**
